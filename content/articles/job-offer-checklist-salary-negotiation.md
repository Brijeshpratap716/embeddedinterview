Getting a job offer is exciting, but don't accept it based solely on salary, designation or company reputation. Before making a decision, check these seven things.

## 1. Research and negotiate your salary

Check [Levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/), [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/) and [AmbitionBox](https://www.ambitionbox.com/) for compensation at the same company, location and job level.

For example, if your current salary is ₹22 LPA and you receive ₹28 LPA while comparable roles pay ₹35–45 LPA, you may be getting lowballed despite a significant hike.

Negotiate based on your market value, skills and experience, not just your current salary.

## 2. Check layoff risks

Research recent layoffs, hiring freezes and restructuring on [Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/), [Fishbowl](https://www.fishbowlapp.com/) and [Layoffs.fyi](https://layoffs.fyi/).

Check whether your specific team is expanding, whether the project is funded and whether the position is new or a replacement. Verify employee rumours against credible news and official announcements. Future layoffs cannot be predicted with certainty.

## 3. Understand your actual role

If you want to remain a hands-on developer, don't rely solely on the job title. Ask your future manager:

- What will my day-to-day activities be?
- How much time will I spend coding?
- Will I develop new features, fix bugs and own software modules?
- How much work involves validation, customer support or documentation?
- What technologies will I work on?

A development designation does not always mean development work.

## 4. Understand the complete compensation

Check base salary, performance bonus, RSUs, ESOPs, vesting schedules and joining bonus repayment conditions.

A ₹36 LPA offer with ₹32 lakh base salary may be more attractive than a ₹40 LPA offer with ₹25 lakh base salary and uncertain variable compensation. Compare recurring annual compensation, not just first-year CTC.

## 5. Check career growth and work culture

Research your future manager, team culture, work-life balance, promotion opportunities and internal job level. Speak with current or former employees if possible.

Choose a role that strengthens your technical skills and provides meaningful ownership.

## 6. Read the offer letter

Verify probation, notice period, termination conditions, employment bonds, bonus clawbacks, intellectual property clauses, work location and working hours. For international offers, confirm visa sponsorship and relocation support.

## 7. Do not resign prematurely

Obtain the final written offer, confirm the joining date and clarify pending approvals, background checks and visa requirements before resigning. Even written offers can carry risks.

## Conclusion

An interview is a two-way evaluation. The company evaluates your skills, and you should evaluate the company.

**Negotiate your market salary, investigate job stability and ensure the actual role aligns with your long-term career goals before accepting.**
