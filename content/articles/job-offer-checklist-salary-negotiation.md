Getting a job offer is an exciting milestone, especially after weeks or months of interview preparation. However, receiving an offer does not mean you should immediately accept it. A higher salary, attractive designation or famous company does not guarantee technical growth, job security or a healthy work environment.

This guide is particularly useful for software engineers, embedded developers, firmware engineers, Linux kernel developers and semiconductor professionals.

## 1. Research the market salary

Do not evaluate an offer solely by the percentage increase over your current salary. Your compensation should reflect your skills, experience, responsibilities and market value.

| Platform | What to research |
|---|---|
| [Levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/) | Company-specific compensation, levels, equity and bonuses |
| [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/) | Salary estimates and employee reviews |
| [AmbitionBox](https://www.ambitionbox.com/) | Salary comparisons, particularly in India |
| [Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/) | Employee compensation and offer discussions |
| [Fishbowl](https://www.fishbowlapp.com/) | Professional and workplace discussions |

Compare recent reports for the same company, location, job family and internal level. Check whether figures represent base salary or total annual compensation. Self-reported data can be incomplete, so use multiple sources.

## 2. Negotiate if you are being lowballed

Suppose your current annual compensation is ₹22 lakh, the offer is ₹28 lakh and comparable roles pay ₹35–45 lakh. Although the offer represents approximately a 27% increase, it may still be below market. These figures are hypothetical and assume comparable compensation components.

Negotiate based on market evidence, technical expertise and responsibilities rather than your previous salary alone.

You can tell HR:

> Thank you for extending the offer. I am excited about the opportunity. Based on my experience, technical expertise and compensation benchmarks for comparable positions at this level, I was expecting compensation in the range of ₹35–40 LPA. Would it be possible to revisit the package and bring it closer to the market range?

Ask whether base salary, equity, joining bonus or job level can be improved if the initial offer is constrained. Never fabricate competing offers. Negotiation is not risk-free, and the employer may decline.

## 3. Understand the complete compensation structure

A higher CTC does not necessarily mean a higher take-home salary.

| Component | What to verify |
|---|---|
| Base salary | Guaranteed annual salary |
| Performance bonus | Target, eligibility and payout history |
| RSUs | Vesting schedule, share value and tax treatment |
| ESOPs | Exercise price, liquidity, expiry and dilution |
| Joining or retention bonus | Repayment and service conditions |
| Relocation allowance | Expenses covered and clawback |
| PF and gratuity | Employer contributions included in CTC |
| Insurance | Employee and family coverage |

For example, Company A offers ₹40 LPA consisting of ₹25 lakh base, ₹5 lakh target bonus and ₹10 lakh annual equity. Company B offers ₹36 LPA consisting of ₹32 lakh base, ₹2 lakh target bonus and ₹2 lakh annual equity. Company B has ₹7 lakh more annual base salary despite the lower headline package.

Compare first-year and recurring compensation over three to four years. Do not treat an entire multi-year stock grant as annual compensation. Stock prices and variable bonuses can change.

## 4. Investigate layoffs and restructuring

Before accepting, investigate whether the company is financially stable, undergoing restructuring, cancelling projects or reducing its workforce. Even large technology and semiconductor companies can hire in one department while laying off employees in another.

Useful sources include [Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/), [Fishbowl](https://www.fishbowlapp.com/), [Layoffs.fyi](https://layoffs.fyi/), Glassdoor, Reddit and LinkedIn.

Search for the company name alongside terms such as layoffs, upcoming layoffs, hiring freeze, restructuring, project cancellation, team stability and offer rescinded.

Blind and Fishbowl may reveal employee concerns before they appear in mainstream news. However, anonymous posts may be speculative or inaccurate. Cross-check dates, business units and locations with official announcements, financial reports, credible news and current employees. No source can guarantee that future layoffs will not happen.

## 5. Check the stability of your specific team

A successful company does not mean every team is equally stable. Ask the hiring manager:

- Is this a newly created position or a replacement?
- Is the headcount approved and the project funded?
- Is the team expanding or shrinking?
- What is the product roadmap for the next one to two years?
- Has the team experienced restructuring recently?
- What happens if the project is cancelled?
- Is employment permanent or dependent on a customer contract?

A strategically important, funded product may offer better stability than an experimental project, but neither guarantees job security.

## 6. Get clarity about your actual role

A title such as Senior Software Engineer or Embedded Software Engineer does not guarantee hands-on development.

| Expected development work | Possible role mismatch |
|---|---|
| Linux kernel and device driver development | Manual validation only |
| BSP and firmware programming | Customer ticket handling only |
| Feature implementation | Log analysis without code ownership |
| System architecture and design | Documentation and reporting only |
| Code reviews and debugging | Project coordination only |

All these activities have professional value. The issue is whether they align with your career goals. If you want to remain a developer, spending years in a position with little coding or technical ownership may make a future transition into development harder.

## 7. Ask about day-to-day activities

Request a discussion with your future manager or technical lead before accepting.

- What does a typical working day look like?
- Approximately what percentage of my time involves coding?
- Which languages, tools and platforms will I use?
- Will I develop new features or maintain existing software?
- Will I own software modules and participate in design and code reviews?
- How much time involves testing, debugging, customer support and meetings?
- Will I have access to source code?
- What are the expected deliverables in my first three to six months?

For a Linux kernel role, clarify whether you will write kernel patches, develop drivers, perform board bring-up or primarily reproduce customer issues. Debugging and validation are normal parts of development; clarify whether you will also implement fixes and own the code.

Do not rely solely on the designation or job description. Ask for important commitments in writing.

## 8. Check the internal job level

Companies use different engineering hierarchies. A Senior Software Engineer at one company may have responsibilities equivalent to a different level elsewhere.

Understand your internal level, compensation band, promotion criteria, next level and individual contributor versus management track. Levels.fyi can help compare levels, although mappings are approximate. Accepting a lower level may affect responsibilities and future compensation growth.

## 9. Research the manager and team culture

Different teams within the same company can have very different working environments. Ask current or former employees about technical freedom, micromanagement, performance evaluations, deadlines, weekend work, attrition and promotions.

Try connecting with people from the same department on LinkedIn. Ask about their experience respectfully and compare several perspectives rather than relying on a single anonymous review.

## 10. Evaluate technical learning and career growth

Ask whether this role will make you a more capable engineer after two or three years.

For embedded and system software engineers, consider opportunities in Linux kernel and drivers, ARM or RISC-V, firmware and BSP, RTOS, GPU software, virtualization, performance optimization, architecture and open-source development.

Check whether you will have mentorship, design ownership and opportunities to solve challenging problems. A slightly lower-paying role with strong technical ownership can sometimes provide greater long-term value than a higher-paying role with limited growth.

## 11. Read the offer letter carefully

Do not assume that verbal promises form part of your employment agreement.

| Clause | What to verify |
|---|---|
| Probation | Duration and termination conditions |
| Notice period | Obligations for both parties |
| Employment bond | Minimum service and repayment terms |
| Bonus clawback | Repayment if you leave early or are terminated |
| Non-compete | Restrictions on future employment |
| Intellectual property | Ownership of personal and side projects |
| Termination | Notice, severance and applicable conditions |
| Work location | Mandatory relocation or office attendance |
| Working hours | Shifts, weekends and on-call duties |
| Employment type | Permanent, contractual or third-party payroll |

Pay particular attention to intellectual property and outside-work clauses if you maintain a personal website, contribute to open source or develop independent software. Employment laws vary by jurisdiction; seek qualified legal advice for unclear or significant restrictions.

## 12. Evaluate work-life balance and location

Consider commuting, rent, working hours, remote or hybrid policies, leave, healthcare and family relocation costs. Compare disposable income rather than gross salary alone.

For international offers, verify visa sponsorship, work permits, taxation, social security, relocation support, dependent visas and what happens to your immigration status if employment ends. Obtain clarity about who pays immigration costs and whether repayment conditions apply.

## 13. Do not resign prematurely

Avoid resigning based solely on a verbal offer. Obtain the formal written offer, confirm compensation and joining date, and understand outstanding approvals, background checks and other conditions.

For international roles, clarify immigration requirements before making irreversible commitments. Even written offers can sometimes be withdrawn. Maintain an emergency fund where possible and understand the remaining risks.

## 14. Compare offers objectively

Rate each offer from 1 to 10 against the factors below. Adjust the illustrative weights according to your priorities.

| Factor | Suggested weight |
|---|---:|
| Compensation | 20% |
| Job security | 20% |
| Hands-on development | 20% |
| Technical learning | 10% |
| Manager and culture | 10% |
| Work-life balance | 10% |
| Career growth | 5% |
| Location | 5% |

Multiply each rating by its weight and add the results to obtain a score out of 10. A higher score is not a reason to ignore a serious contractual, immigration or job-security concern.

## 15. Final checklist before accepting

- [ ] Researched market salary using Levels.fyi and other sources.
- [ ] Negotiated compensation where appropriate.
- [ ] Verified base salary, bonuses and complete CTC.
- [ ] Understood RSU or ESOP vesting and repayment conditions.
- [ ] Researched layoffs, restructuring and financial stability.
- [ ] Checked Blind and Fishbowl discussions and corroborated claims.
- [ ] Confirmed team funding and project stability.
- [ ] Discussed day-to-day responsibilities with the hiring manager.
- [ ] Confirmed hands-on development and code ownership.
- [ ] Verified internal job level and promotion opportunities.
- [ ] Researched team culture and work-life balance.
- [ ] Reviewed probation, notice, termination and clawback clauses.
- [ ] Checked intellectual property and outside-work restrictions.
- [ ] Confirmed visa and relocation arrangements, if applicable.
- [ ] Received the final written offer and clarified outstanding conditions.

## Conclusion

An interview is a two-way evaluation. The company evaluates your technical abilities, and you should evaluate its compensation, stability, management, culture and growth opportunities.

Negotiate according to your market value. Research the company before joining. Understand your actual responsibilities rather than trusting the designation alone.

**Choose a job that pays you fairly, provides reasonable stability, strengthens your technical abilities and brings you closer to your long-term career goals.**
