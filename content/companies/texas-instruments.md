## How the process usually runs

Much of TI's embedded hiring in India is through campus recruiting. Candidates describe an online assessment with C programming, embedded concepts, electronics MCQs and debugging problems, followed by one or two technical interviews on C, the projects on the resume, microcontrollers and computer architecture, then an HR round asking why you chose an embedded role. US candidates describe single rounds mixing behavioural questions with technical questions drawn from their projects.

## Topics that come up most

- **C:** pointers, dynamic memory, "guess the output" → [C Programming](../../topics/c-programming/), [Pointers & Memory](../../topics/pointers-memory/)
- **Interrupts and microcontrollers** → [Embedded C](../../topics/embedded-c/), [Microcontrollers & ARM](../../topics/microcontrollers-arm/)
- **Bit manipulation:** XOR for parity, shifts and masks → [Bit Manipulation](../../topics/bit-manipulation/)
- **Architecture:** buses and caching → [Microcontrollers & ARM](../../topics/microcontrollers-arm/)
- **Projects:** how you interfaced a board with motors or sensors → [Communication Protocols](../../topics/communication-protocols/), [Electronics & Hardware](../../topics/electronics-hardware/)

## Questions candidates reported (paraphrased)

- Predict the output of this C snippet; questions on pointers and dynamic memory allocation.
- Explain how an interrupt is handled.
- Use XOR to compute parity; shift and mask questions.
- Write a sorting algorithm in C.
- Which protocol did you use to connect your development board to the motor driver, and why?
- Why an embedded role rather than a general software role?

## How to prepare

1. Know your own projects deeply, down to the peripherals, protocols and registers; TI interviewers dig into them.
2. Practise C output questions and pointer tracing.
3. Revise interrupt handling and microcontroller basics (timers, ADC, GPIO), ideally on an MSP430 or another TI LaunchPad if you have one.
